\exercise{P5-1}
The pipe is hoisted using the tongs. If the coefficient of static friction at $A$ and $B$ is $\mu_s$, determine the smallest dimension $b$ so that any pipe of inner diameter $d$ can be lifted.
\begin{figure} [ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.15\linewidth]{./figures/P5_1_1.png}
\end{figure}
\bigbreak
\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \incfig[0.3]{P5_1}
  \caption{Free-body diagram of the right tong half}
  \label{fig:P5-1}
\end{figure}
We consider one half of the tong, in this case the right half as shown on \autoref{fig:P5-1}. For static equilibrium to occur we have the necessary and sufficient conditions:
\begin{align*}
  F_{R_x} &= \sum F_x = 0 \\
  F_{R_y} &= \sum F_y = 0 \\
  M_{R_C} &= \sum M_C = 0
.\end{align*}
For a couple moment not to arise, the lines of actions of all the forces must intersect at some common point, $P$. 

At point $B$, right at the verge of slippage, the line of action of the resultant force at $B$ makes the friction angle, $\phi = \tan^{-1} \mu_s$ below the horizontal. Using the equation of a line we get:
\[ 
y + h= \mu_s (x - \frac{d}{2}) \implies y = \mu _s \left( x - \frac{d}{2} \right) - h
.\]
The line of action at point $B$ will lie along this line.

At the top the tong is attached to a vertical chain, such that the force that is transmittet to the tong at the top is vertical, with a line of action of $x = b$. 

As mentioned above, all the lines of actions of the forces must intersect at a common point $P$. This means that the two lines defined above must intersect at this point $P$. This intersection will be:
\[ 
P = \left( b, \mu_s \left( b - \frac{d}{2} \right) - h \right)
.\]
The pin force at $C$ will thus act along the line $CP$. If this point $P$ is below point $C$ a counter clockwise torque is induced and thus the jaws of the tong will tend to open up. Therefore, for the jaws to wedge and hold, the concurrency point $P$ must be at or above $C$ as:
\begin{align*}
  y_p &\geq 0 \\
  \mu_s \left( b - \frac{d}{2} \right) - h &\geq 0 \\
  \implies \mu_s \left( b_{\mathrm{min}} - \frac{d}{2} \right) - h &= 0 \\
  b_{\mathrm{min}} &= \frac{h}{\mu_s} + \frac{d}{2}
.\end{align*}
Therefore the smallest dimension of $b$ such that the pipe can be lifted is $b_{\mathrm{min}} = \frac{h}{\mu_s} + \frac{d}{2}$.
