\exercise{P5-1}
The pipe is hoisted using the tongs. If the coefficient of static friction at $A$ and $B$ is $\mu_s$, determine the smallest dimension $b$ so that any pipe of inner diameter $d$ can be lifted.
\begin{figure} [ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.15\linewidth]{./figures/P5_1_1.png}
\end{figure}
\bigbreak
\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \incfig[0.5]{P5_1}
  \caption{Free-body diagram of the right tong part}
  \label{fig:P5-1}
\end{figure}
The normal conditions for equilibrium states:
\begin{align*}
  F_{R_x} &= 0 \\
  F_{R_y} &= 0 \\
  M_{R_C} &= 0
.\end{align*}
We take the right tong member as a separate (rigid) body as shown on \autoref{fig:P5-1}. It is acted on be three forces:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A chain force $T$ at the upper eye, which acts vertically at $x = b$
  \item A reaction force $R_B$ from the pipe at $B$. As the inner wall of $B$ is vertical the reaction normal force will act horizontally to the left. As the tongs move upwards the friction force on this will point vertically downwards
\end{enumerate}
